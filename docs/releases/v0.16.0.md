# Heliox OS v0.16.0 — To-dos, Watchers, Habits and Quick Answers

v0.16.0 makes Heliox useful for the small things you ask a computer all day. Keep a to-do list by voice, ask Heliox to tell you when an app closes or a download finishes, set reminders that repeat or wait until you open an app, track habits, and say "good morning" or "good night" for the day at a glance. Sums, conversions, dates, world time and questions about your files and your computer's health are answered on the spot. All 91 built-in commands are answered in code, without a model, and none of the new features can act on their own.

## Highlights

- **To-do list** — "Add call the bank to my list", "what's on my list", "mark call the bank as done" and "clear finished items". Open items show in the Today tab, the daily brief and the exported report. "Mark this email as done" still goes to the planner, because "mark … as done" only answers when the list is named or an open item matches.
- **Tell me when** — "Tell me when Chrome closes", "let me know when my download finishes", "tell me when the invoice shows up", "tell me when my battery is full" and "tell me when my computer calms down". Watchers check every few seconds, say once what happened, and give up after a day. They only read process, battery, processor and Downloads-folder state.
- **Repeating and app reminders** — "Remind me every hour to stand up", "remind me every weekday at 9 to check email" and "stop reminding me to stand up". Interval reminders skip 10 PM to 7 AM and keep their cadence after you have been away. "Remind me to submit my timesheet when I open Outlook" and "every time I open Slack, remind me to set my status" wait for that app to come to the front.
- **Good morning and good night** — "Good morning" gives the date, today's reminders, your list, habit streaks to keep and a nearly full drive or low battery. "Good night" sums up the day with focus time, finished to-dos and tomorrow's first reminder.
- **Habits and focus history** — "Log a workout", "I drank a glass of water", "how many workouts this week" and "what's my workout streak". "I did …" only logs habits you already track, so ordinary sentences are left alone. Focus sessions are logged, so "how long did I focus this week" has an answer.
- **Quick answers and helpers** — "What's 15 percent of 240", "convert 5 miles to km", "how many days until Christmas", "how long until 5 pm" and "what time is it in Tokyo" are worked out exactly; arithmetic is parsed, never run as code. "Generate a password" puts one on the clipboard and nowhere else. Coin flips, dice, random picks and a stopwatch need no model.
- **Your files and this computer** — "What did I download today", "what was the last file I downloaded" and "where is my budget spreadsheet" (your own folders, at most a few seconds, never inside code repositories). "Why is my computer slow", "what's using my memory" and "how much disk space do I have" read processor, memory, disks and the biggest apps; closing anything still asks first.
- **Clipboard history, snippets and focus heads-ups** — Opt-in clipboard history in memory only, never passwords, keys or private windows: "what did I copy earlier", "copy the second one back". "Save that as my signature" and "copy my signature" reuse text. "During focus, tell me if I open YouTube" gives one gentle heads-up per session and blocks nothing.

## Reproducible evidence snapshot

- The generated catalog still declares **162 action types** across **21 executable specialists**; **22** actions have an independent post-condition verifier. This does not mean every action is available on every host or independently verified.
- New built-in command suite: every one of the **91** command-guide examples was handled in code (**91/91**) with **1.384 ms median** and **14.111 ms p95** per reply across 1,820 replies, zero model calls and zero actions executed. Stand-ins replace the clipboard, speech, the settings file and machine readings, so this is the control path itself, not speech recognition, text-to-speech or end-to-end voice latency.
- The release benchmark measures **25.234 ms median** and **26.930 ms p95** for 100 guarded, steady-state CPU-status requests with **zero model calls** (v0.15.0: 27.183 ms and 28.257 ms).
- The deterministic dispatch regression passed **59/59** fixed cases with **0.026 ms median** routing latency.
- During a real one-second CPU monitor sample, the concurrent heartbeat's maximum interval was **16.618 ms** across 65 heartbeats. This measures scheduler availability, not UI or hardware-input latency.
- The assistance co-adaptation quick benchmark again ran on **simulated users only**; all three policy agency audits passed. This is not evidence about people.
- The daemon now registers **301 WebSocket RPC methods**.

## Safety and privacy boundaries

- Nothing new widens what Heliox may do on its own. Watchers, the health check, the file finder and focus heads-ups only read; app reminders and focus heads-ups compare the front window and forget it. Closing an app, opening a file or anything else that changes the computer is an ordinary request with the usual approvals.
- Clipboard history is off by default, lives in memory only (never on disk or in a backup) and is forgotten on restart or when turned off. On Windows it opens the clipboard only after the system reports a change. Text that looks like a password, API key, token, private key or card number is never kept, saved as a snippet or read aloud.
- A generated password goes to the clipboard and nowhere else: not the reply, the chat, the disk or clipboard history.
- To-dos, habits, focus history, snippets and app reminders are kept on this computer, listed in Settings → Your data with a Clear button each, and included in backups. Phrases that are only sometimes Heliox's ("copy my …", "where is my …", "I drank …") are answered only when they name a snippet, a kind of file or a habit you track; otherwise they go to the planner or the conversation as before. A voice shortcut you made earlier keeps working even if its phrase is now a built-in (say, "good night"), by voice as well as in chat; new shortcuts cannot take a built-in phrase.
- Camera, gaze, gesture, microphone, speaker, accessibility permissions, first-run setup, and other hardware-dependent paths still require validation on the user's actual devices. Neural intent remains an accessibility and research interface.
- SignPath test-policy signing is validated. Production signing remains dependent on the production certificate becoming usable and the release workflow being migrated to it.

## Upgrade guidance

Download the installer matching the operating system from the [v0.16.0 release](https://github.com/VyomKulshrestha/Heliox-OS/releases/tag/v0.16.0). Back up settings, allow first-run Chromium and daemon setup to complete, and re-check provider CLI login, integration credentials, microphone, camera, browser, local TTS, Air Handoff pairing, peer collaboration, MCP servers and optional model configuration after installation. Your undo history, receipts, notes, reminders and timeline carry over. Clipboard history stays off until you turn it on ("turn on clipboard history" or Settings → Your data); focus history starts with sessions finished after the upgrade.
