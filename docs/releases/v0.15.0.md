# Heliox OS v0.15.0 — Undoable Tasks, Signed Receipts and Your Day

v0.15.0 makes what Heliox does reversible, provable and easy to look back on. Finished tasks can be undone item by item, every task that ran actions leaves a signed receipt, and an opt-in activity timeline powers a Today tab, a daily brief and questions like "what was I working on yesterday afternoon?". It also adds focus sessions, reminders, notes, routines, voice shortcuts, saved app setups, requests from a paired phone, a command palette and a panel that shows everything Heliox keeps. Everything new is local, and none of it widens what Heliox may do on its own.

## Highlights

- **Per-task undo** — Before Heliox writes, moves, copies or deletes a file, replaces the clipboard, sets an environment variable or changes the volume or brightness, it keeps what it needs to put the change back. Each finished task shows **Undo**; the preview lists what will be put back, what changed since (left alone, never overwritten) and what can never be undone, such as a sent email, and **Show what changed** gives a line-by-line diff of each file. "Undo the last two tasks" opens each preview in turn. Undo is computed in code, never by a model, and every undo needs your click. The approval dialog now marks each action **Can undo** or **Can't undo**.
- **Signed task receipts** — Every task that ran actions leaves an Ed25519-signed, hash-chained receipt of what ran, what you approved and what happened; file contents and outputs appear only as hashes. Verify a receipt from the task in chat, check the whole chain in Settings, or check an exported receipt anywhere with `python -m pilot.workflows.task_receipts verify`. "What did you do today?" and "when did I ask you to email the invoice?" are answered from them.
- **Your day** — The opt-in activity timeline records which app and window title were in front, never pixels or screen text, and skips password managers and private windows. The **Today** tab shows what Heliox ran, where your time went and what can still be undone, for today, yesterday or the week, and exports a Markdown report. An opt-in daily brief arrives at the time you choose, and usage nudges tell you once a day when an app passes your limit.
- **Focus, reminders, notes and timers** — "Focus for 45 minutes", "start a pomodoro" and quiet hours ("focus every weekday from 9 to 12") hold reminders and the brief and keep Heliox quiet, while approvals and answers still come through. "Remind me tomorrow at 9 to send the report", "set a timer for 10 minutes", "note that the plumber comes Tuesday" and "where did you put my report?" are handled right away, without planning.
- **Routines, voice shortcuts and setups** — "Do that every day at 9" turns a finished task into a routine that may use only the actions that task used, and you confirm it on screen. "When I say start work, open VS Code and Slack" creates a voice shortcut, "save this as my coding setup" remembers your open apps, and "do that again" repeats the last task with fresh approvals.
- **Requests from your phone** — A paired Air Handoff phone can send an encrypted, signed request. It waits for **Run** on the computer; if you allow it, status checks start at once with a fixed read-only list. The answer goes back to the phone, which can never approve anything.
- **Command palette and Your data** — Ctrl+K or "what can I say?" lists every built-in command with examples that a test runs as written. Settings → Your data shows everything Heliox keeps on this computer with a Clear button for each; "forget my activity from the last hour" removes that stretch, and backups never include settings, tokens or keys.

## Reproducible evidence snapshot

- The generated catalog declares **162 action types** across **21 executable specialists** with provider coverage for every declared action; **22** actions have an independent post-condition verifier. This does not mean every action is available on every host or independently verified.
- The release benchmark measures **27.183 ms median** and **28.257 ms p95** for 100 guarded, steady-state CPU-status requests with **zero model calls**.
- The deterministic dispatch regression passed **59/59** fixed cases with **0.023 ms median** routing latency.
- During a real one-second CPU monitor sample, the concurrent heartbeat's maximum interval was **16.237 ms** across 64 heartbeats. This measures scheduler availability, not UI or hardware-input latency.
- The assistance co-adaptation quick benchmark again ran on **simulated users only**; all three policy agency audits passed. This is not evidence about people.
- Every example in the new command guide is run through the real handlers by `tests/test_command_guide.py`, and the daemon now registers **276 WebSocket RPC methods**.

## Safety and privacy boundaries

- Undo restores only what Heliox recorded, re-checks each item right before restoring it, and leaves anything you changed since alone. Effects that leave the computer, and system changes such as shell commands or package installs, are never undoable, and the approval dialog says so up front. Clipboard text and earlier environment values are kept in memory only.
- Receipts describe what happened; they never grant, approve or run anything. When no signing key can be stored, receipts are written unsigned and say so.
- The activity timeline, daily brief and usage nudges are off by default. The timeline keeps app names and window titles only, and no model sees it: questions about your day are answered in code.
- Focus sessions never hold approvals, task results, errors or answers. Phone requests are off by default, and a phone can never approve or deny anything. Only everyday settings (the timeline and the brief) can change by voice; security, gateway, approval and model settings cannot.
- Undo copies, receipts and the timeline live in folders that actions may not read or change. Backups contain only your personal stores; restoring reads only known file names from a Heliox backup in your Downloads folder.
- Camera, gaze, gesture, microphone, speaker, accessibility permissions, first-run setup, and other hardware-dependent paths still require validation on the user's actual devices. Neural intent remains an accessibility and research interface.
- SignPath test-policy signing is validated. Production signing remains dependent on the production certificate becoming usable and the release workflow being migrated to it.

## Upgrade guidance

Download the installer matching the operating system from the [v0.15.0 release](https://github.com/VyomKulshrestha/Heliox-OS/releases/tag/v0.15.0). Back up settings, allow first-run Chromium and daemon setup to complete, and re-check provider CLI login, integration credentials, microphone, camera, browser, local TTS, Air Handoff pairing, peer collaboration, MCP servers and optional model configuration after installation. Undo history and task receipts start with tasks run after the upgrade. The activity timeline, daily brief, usage nudges and phone requests stay off until you turn them on.
